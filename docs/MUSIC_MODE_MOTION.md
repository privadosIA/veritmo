# Music motion: animated pieces that follow a song

A **piece** is a motion-graphics video made to go with a song: typography, shapes and (optionally) illustrated characters that move on the beat, with the words of the lyrics appearing as they are sung. It is drawn by Chrome from HTML pages that the server builds itself from a **catalog of animated parts**; no video model is used and the same piece always gives the same frames.

It is the same machinery as the animated graphics of a scene (see [SCENES_YAML.md](SCENES_YAML.md#motion-graphics-and-overlays)), with three additions: the pages know the song (its beats, sections and words), the model chooses and fills parts instead of writing pages, and the whole video follows one design.

## How a piece is made

1. **The song is read into a map**: tempo, the time of every beat (followed in the audio: a song made by a model does not keep an exact tempo), the sections of the lyrics (`[Verse]`, `[Chorus]`...) with their times, every word with its time, and the loudness every quarter of a second. By default the lines of the lyrics are spread over their sections, so the times of the words are an estimate (`song_map.words_estimated` is `true`). Ask for the words to be **heard** (`transcribe: true` when the piece is created, or `POST /api/music-motion/pieces/{id}/transcribe` afterwards) and Whisper, by word and on the CPU (about a third of the song's length), gives the time of each one: the words stay as you wrote them, the heard words only give the times (a word Whisper hears another way, such as `9` for `nine`, or not at all, takes a time between its neighbours), every section starts where its first word is sung and `words_estimated` is `false`; `song_map.words_source` says where the times come from (`estimated`, `heard`, `heard-voice` or `edited`) and each word says whether it was heard and how sure Whisper was. Without written lyrics the lines are made from what is heard, cut at the pauses of the voice. A word that is still off can be moved by hand (`PATCH .../words`); see [Timing the words](#timing-the-words). A script nobody edited is made again from the new sections; one you edited is kept and the answer warns that it may need adjusting.
2. **A design** is chosen: two tones (a dark and a light one), an accent colour, two fonts, a mode (light or dark) for each section tag, a one-sentence concept and, optionally, two finishes for the text: `verse_style` (`plain` by default; `highlight` puts a background in the accent colour on the word being sung and keeps it on the ones already sung, so the verse is marked up to the word of the moment; `karaoke` turns the words already sung to the accent colour; `glow` shows each word in the text colour with a soft glow and a halo, with no background) and `hit_style` (`plain` by default; `metal`, a metallic gradient with a shine that crosses the letters, and `glow`, letters in the accent colour with a light around them; in both the letters enter one after another). They use the time of each word, so they are as exact as it is (heard words are exact, estimated ones are not). The assistant proposes it from the style and the lyrics; with no assistant, a fixed palette is chosen by the words of the style. You can edit every value; a design you set is never overwritten by a new song.
3. **A script** lists the pieces of the video, each with its part, parameters, the lyric lines it covers (with a keyword painted in the accent colour) and, for a chorus, a *word hit* (one word at full screen). The cuts are moved to the nearest beat. The script is a JSON document you can read and edit (`GET` and `PUT /api/music-motion/pieces/{id}/script`; the piece keeps it in the database), and an edited script is validated again before it is used.

**The mode follows the design.** A piece carries no `mode` unless someone set one: its page is drawn in the mode the design gives its section (`modes[tag]`, then `default_mode`), so changing the design changes every piece that does not have a mode of its own. The proposals (the assistant's and the fixed rule) write none; a person, or an assistant with a reason (a light chorus on a dark design), may set it. Saving a design with other modes answers a `notice` counting the pieces that carry a mode of their own that differs from the design's (nothing is changed); to make them follow the design, save the script again without their `mode`.
A piece of the script can also carry a `design` of its own (any of `dark`, `light`, `accent`, `display`, `body`, `verse_style` and `hit_style`, which replace those of the song's design for that piece only, so an era can have its own palette; the result is validated as a whole design) and `with`, up to three extra parts drawn together with its main part (for example a strip of years that stays while the main screen changes; no part can be in a piece twice). The page of a piece also knows where the piece starts in the song and how long the song is (`window.__scene.song_start`, `song_length`), so a part can draw the position of the song.

A parameter can also **change over a piece**. A piece, and each `with` entry, may carry `animate`: `{"<parameter>": [{"t": 0, "value": 0}, {"t": 4, "value": 100, "ease": "out"}]}`, with `t` in seconds from the start of the piece and `ease` one of `linear` (the default), `in`, `out`, `in-out` or `step`; it is the ease of the segment that starts at that keyframe. Between two keyframes a number is interpolated (`in` is `p³`, `out` is `1-(1-p)³`, `in-out` the cubic that does both, `step` holds the earlier value), an `int` is rounded after interpolating, and any value that is not a number is held until the next keyframe; before the first keyframe and after the last the value is the nearest one. Only a parameter the manifest marks `"animatable": true` can move (the catalog says so in each parameter): the part reads it from `window.__params` inside `__partSeek`, never while it loads, and a parameter that the fragment writes as text (`{{name}}`) cannot be animatable (a manifest that says so is left out of the list; a drafted part never declares one). The built-in animatable parameters are `counter` (`from`, `step`), `timeline-strip` (`progress`), `achievement-toast` (`progress`), `password-field` (`strength`) and `particles` (`streak`). The catalog's `features` lists `animate` for a dashboard that wants to know before it saves. Golden values for a port of the evaluation: `[{t:0,v:0,ease:out},{t:4,v:100}]` gives 0 at 0 s, 57.8125 at 1 s, 100 at 4 s and after; `[{t:0,v:0},{t:4,v:100,ease:in}]` read at 2 s gives 50 (the ease belongs to the earlier keyframe, so the last one's is ignored); `[{t:0,v:0,ease:in},{t:4,v:100}]` at 2 s gives 12.5; a `step` or a string keeps the earlier value until the next keyframe. The preview pages and the render evaluate it the same way, and a page with nothing animated is the page it always was.

A piece can also be a **3D scene** drawn by Blender instead of a page: `{"id": "c1", "start": 30, "end": 42, "blender": {"description": {…}}}`, with no `part` and none of the fields of a page (`params`, `animate`, `with`, `design`, `mode`, `lines`, `hit`, `stage`: `422` naming the piece and the field). The description is the JSON of a scene (see `docs/SCENES_YAML.md`), checked when the script is saved and when it renders as a hand-written one for a scene as long as the piece, with the song's tempo (a key may be in beats, counted from the **start of the piece**, so it lands on the song's beats only when the piece starts on one; the writer is told how many seconds after the start the song's first beat falls) and the song's design (`bg`, `fg`, `accent`, and the `display` and `body` fonts when the design has one in the server's pack); no font files. The render writes it as a scene `blender: {build: <id>.scene.json}` of the piece's project, with the song's tempo and design in `defaults.motion`, so crossfades and the soundtrack work as for the other pieces; it needs Blender (`503` with how to install it) and a script of 3D pieces only does not need the browser. The preview lists it with `blender: true` and no page, `GET .../frame?t=` draws it with Blender on the CPU, the review leaves it out (`skipped`), and the render estimate counts it by its length and frame. `POST .../script/{piece}/blender {idea, revise?, description?, provider?, save?}` writes or changes it with a model and puts it in the script; `POST .../script/{piece}/blender/frame {description, t, width?}` draws a description that is not saved. The catalog's `features` lists `blender-motion`. The proposal of a script never chooses a 3D piece. A 3D piece can also place **3D models** (`.glb` characters and props with their own animations, see "Models from files" in `docs/SCENES_YAML.md`): the piece keeps them in its project (`PUT /api/music-motion/pieces/{id}/models/{name}` uploads a `.glb`, read before it is stored, or a picture its textures name, such as the `colormap.png` that Kenney's characters need; `GET .../models` lists them with their `clips`, `triangles` and `needs`; `DELETE` removes one, refused with `409` while a 3D piece of the saved script places it or needs it), and an object `{"type": "model", "file": "ana.glb", "size": 1.7, "animation": {"clip": "sprint"}}` of a piece's description is checked against them when the script is saved, written, previewed and rendered (`422` naming the piece and the place in the JSON for a file, a picture or a clip the piece does not have). The writer of a 3D piece is told the piece's models and their clips. Font files are still refused (a piece takes the design's fonts). The catalog's `features` lists `blender-models` for this. `POST /api/music-motion/pieces/{id}/models` with `{"from": "<pack>/<model>"}` copies a model of the library (`docs/MODEL_LIBRARY.md`) into the piece, with its textures.

A script can also have **layers**: up to four lanes of parts drawn over the whole sequence of pieces, so a strip that stays across several pieces is one item instead of an extra in each. `"layers": [{"id": "years", "items": [{"part": "timeline-strip", "params": {…}, "start": 0, "end": 212.4, "animate": {…}}]}]`, with `start` and `end` in seconds of the song and up to 32 items per layer. Each item is drawn in the page of every piece its range touches and shows only between its `start` and `end`; the lanes are drawn in order, the last on top, over the parts of the pieces and under the verse and the word hit. The part of an item is told its time as seconds **from the item's `start`** (and `animate` keyframes of an item are read in that same time), so a part that draws from `t` carries on across the cuts between pieces; its beat clock, like any part's, is the piece's own (the song map is cut per piece), so a part that counts beats starts again in each piece. Items of one lane follow one another and do not overlap; lanes may overlap each other. A page draws each part once (an item cannot use the part that is a piece's main part or one of its extras, or the part of another item showing in the same piece) and at most 8 parts. The level does not swap a part of a layer, as it does not swap an extra. The render estimate adds the cost of the layer parts a piece draws. The catalog's `features` lists `layers`. An item may name a whole HyperFrames **block or component** (`"block": "lower-third-bild"`, see [HYPERFRAMES.md](HYPERFRAMES.md)) instead of a `part`; its `params` are then the values of the variables the block declares, and it cannot `animate`. The block is drawn as it is stored inside an iframe of the page of every piece its range touches (so it stays under the layers above it and under the verse), its own timeline is sought to the seconds from the item's `start`, and the render, the frame route and the reviewer serve it its files and the pinned libraries, with nothing fetched from outside. A script that names a block that is not in the catalog, failed its checks or lacks the libraries is refused when it is saved. The script writer is offered (`blocks: "auto"`, the default of the propose request; `"off"` leaves them out), when blocks are installed and checked, a menu of up to 40 usable blocks and components (state `ok`, libraries and runtime installed) and may add up to two layers of them (up to 8 items each, with the variables the block declares); the server keeps only the valid items (the block is in the menu, the range is inside the song, items of a lane do not overlap) and names what it dropped in the `notes` of the proposal, and a script that fails only because of its block layers is kept without them. Measured on 2026-11-17 with gemma4:31b on a 60 s song and a menu of 40: asked as "you MAY add" it named no block in 2 of 2 runs, asked to add one or two when a block fits it named two valid ones in 3 of 3 (a title element over the opening and one over the chorus, with its text in the variables), with no item dropped, and took 78 to 91 s against 73 s without the menu. The preview pages (`POST .../preview`) carry the iframe but a browser cannot serve it the block's files: use `GET .../pieces/{id}/frame`, which draws it with the server's Chrome. Measured on a real piece of 24 pieces and 135 s, with a `timeline-strip` over all of them: building all the pages took 153 ms and 339 kB against 82 ms and 263 kB without it, and a frame of a page took 62 ms either way (the strip adds less than the noise of the measure).

4. **The piece is drawn** as a project of its own with one `motion_graphic` scene per piece of the script, the song as the soundtrack. Scenes whose piece did not change come from the cache, so editing one piece draws one scene again.

## The choices

| | |
|---|---|
| **Style** | `typographic` (default) or `illustrated` (characters on a stage, 24 fps by default). |
| **Level** | `simple` (flat parts, cuts and entrances on the beat; the one that is best synchronised), `medium` (depth and parallax) or `advanced` (3D wireframe, glitch, particles, motion blur). A part that the level does not support is replaced by a simpler one and the answer says so. |
| **Frame rate** | 24, 30 or 60. |
| **Text** | Two layers: the verse that is being sung at the bottom (its words appear when they are sung) and, optionally, a word hit at full screen. |

Render time is a forecast before it starts (`estimate`, always marked as one) and is corrected by what the first renders on your machine took. Measured on one machine at 1280x720: about 55 ms a frame for the simple parts, 85 ms for the 3D and canvas ones, so a 4-minute song at 30 fps is about 7 minutes at `simple` and 10 at `advanced`, and about 20 at `advanced` and 60 fps. The advanced level is optional for that reason.

## The parts that come with the program

`toggle`, `glass-card`, `stamp`, `counter`, `terminal`, `flat-slabs` (simple), `parallax-cards`, `depth-stack` (medium), `wire-sphere`, `glitch-text`, `particles` (advanced), the interface parts `search-box` (a phrase typed letter by letter with a suggestion more on every beat), `click-button` (a cursor that comes and presses it on a beat), `roll-counter` (digits that roll from one number to another, with an optional glitch at zero), `achievement-toast` (a notice with a progress bar that drops in and leaves), `data-table`, `dependency-tree` and `password-field` (a dot per beat and a strength bar), the pop background `sunburst`, and `blank` (draws nothing of its own: the piece is its stage). The interface parts take their text as parameters (lists as `a|b|c`, rows as `a|b;c|d`) so a fixed rule or a model only has to fill them. Measured at 1280x720 a frame of each costs 52 to 65 ms, except the sunburst (90 ms, its conic gradient is repainted every frame). `GET /api/music-motion/catalog` lists them with their parameters and limits. The era-pack parts, for pieces that change their look with the song: `timeline-strip` (a bar of marks with a cursor that follows the position in the song; manual), `phosphor-terminal` (green or amber CRT terminal typed on the beat), `oscilloscope` (a Lissajous figure that turns and pulses), `synth-sun` (a striped sun over a grid in perspective), `warp-grid` (a mesh bent by a point of light with a trail), `nebula` (seeded clouds of particles), `word-cloud` (words that appear over the beats inside the frame) and `network-diagram` (layers of nodes that light up one by one). Measured at 1280x720 a frame of each costs 77 to 99 ms (the nebula is the slowest, its radial gradients are repainted every frame).

The fragment and manifest of any part, these included, can be read with `GET /api/music-motion/parts/{name}` (`source: builtin`), so an editor can draw a piece in the browser with the real parts.

### Where the state lives

The state of this mode is in the database of the server (SQLite by default; see [DATA_STORE.md](DATA_STORE.md)), not in folders of the settings: the parts of the user (`mm_parts`), the pieces with their six documents (design, script, song map, proposal, scene batches and review: `mm_pieces`, `mm_piece_documents`, the size of the piece kept with it), the drafts of parts (`mm_part_drafts`), the references, learnings and styles (`mm_references`, `mm_learnings`, `mm_presets`), the times the renders of the machine took (`mm_render_ratios`, which the estimate of a piece starts from) and the user's characters and backgrounds (`mm_library_items`, `mm_library_files`). What stays in files is media: the song and its stems, the key frames of a reference, the picture and preview frames of a draft, the frames and the video of a render, and what a render reads by path (`scenes.yaml`, `out/`, `tmp/`). Approving the scenes of a batch, approving a draft and deleting a piece are each one change in a transaction: all of it or nothing. A proposal, a batch of scenes, a draft, a reference or a learning that was running when the server stopped is marked as interrupted when it starts.

Because the view of a piece reads its parts with one query and its size from its row, `GET /api/music-motion/pieces` and `GET .../pieces/{id}` take tens of milliseconds where they took 75 s and 8 s with the parts read from folders (measured 2026-11-18 on a piece of 34 script pieces: 40 ms and 44 ms).

### Adding a part

The parts of the user live in the database (see "Where the state lives"). Add one with `POST /api/music-motion/parts {manifest, fragment}` (the same checks as the approval of a draft: `422` with the problems, `409` for a name that is taken or is the name of a part that comes with the program) and it appears in the next list, with no restart. The part is two texts, the manifest and the fragment, which `GET /api/music-motion/parts/{name}` reads back and `PUT` replaces; a part of the user with the name of a built-in one is refused.

A folder put by hand in `~/.config/veritmo/music-motion/parts/<name>/` is **not read any more**: the server imports the folders that exist when it starts, once, and `veritmo data import music-motion/parts` brings in the ones that came later (it never changes a part that is already in, and never touches the files).

```
parts/
  my-part/
    manifest.json
    part.html
```

`manifest.json`:

```json
{"name": "my-part", "description": "What it shows and how it moves", "version": 1, "cost_ms": 60,
 "levels": ["simple", "medium", "advanced"],
 "params": [
   {"name": "label", "type": "string", "default": "hello", "max_length": 20},
   {"name": "count", "type": "int", "default": 3, "min": 1, "max": 6}]}
```

Parameter types are `string`, `int`, `number`, `bool` and `enum` (with `options`); every parameter needs a default. `cost_ms` is what a frame takes to draw (it feeds the forecast); `"manual": true` keeps the part out of what the program chooses by itself.

`part.html` is a fragment: HTML, CSS and a script. `{{name}}` is replaced by the parameter, escaped for HTML; `{{json:name}}` by its JSON value. The script reads `window.__params`, and draws by defining `window.__partSeek = function (t, beat) {...}`, which is called before each frame with the time in seconds and the beat clock:

```
beat = {number, position, section, sectionIndex, bpm, time}
```

`number` is the index of the last beat (-1 before the first), `position` how far inside it the frame is (0 up to 1) and `section` the tag of the lyrics. `beat.word` and `beat.line` say which written word and which line are being sung at that frame, in the seconds of the page like `beat.time`: `word = {text, index, line, start, end, position}` (`index` is its place in its line, `line` the line's place in `window.__scene.lines`, `position` runs from 0 at its start to 1 at its end) and `line = {index, text, start, end, position}`; each is `null` when nothing is sung (a break between lines, or the silence between two words for `word`). The words of `window.__scene.lines[].words[]` carry `heard` (`false` for one whose time was made up between its neighbours or moved by hand), so a part can show an unsure word apart. A part that ignores all of this is unchanged. Draw from them and the frames land on the music; do not use the wall clock or `Math.random()` (use a seeded generator). The colours and fonts of the design are CSS variables: `var(--bg)`, `var(--fg)`, `var(--accent)`, `var(--display)`, `var(--body)`. A fragment must not load anything from outside and must not set `window.__seek` (the page's own seek runs the verse and the stage). A part that breaks any rule is left out of the list, with one warning in the server log.

A scene's manifest may also carry `"world"`, one line saying where the video happens: the writer of the first scene of a piece that has no world states it there and the batch keeps it with the piece, so the next scenes are written in the same world. A fragment may place a drawing of the design's theme with `{{asset:name}}` and read `window.__theme` (see "The house style"). A manifest may also say `"verse": "own"` and `"hit": "own"` when the part draws the lyric or the word hit itself, from `window.__scene.lines` and `window.__scene.hit` (the page then leaves its own layers out; see "Scenes").

`docs/examples/beat-page.html` is a complete page that reads the beat clock.

### Timelines and clips (GSAP)

A part or a scene may also animate with a GSAP timeline, the contract that [HyperFrames](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes) uses and that the models know. It needs the GSAP core installed (`bash scripts/get-gsap.sh`, see `docs/INSTALL.md` and `THIRD_PARTY.md` for the licence); the page embeds the file inline when a fragment names `gsap` or `__timelines`, so nothing is loaded from the network. A fragment builds ONE paused timeline while it loads and registers it; the page seeks it, on every frame and after `__partSeek`, to the part's own time (the seconds from the start of the scene; for an item of a layer, from the start of the item), without firing callbacks, so the frames are the same on every render:

```html
<div id="box" class="clip" data-start="0.5" data-duration="3"><div id="dot"></div></div>
<script>
  var tl = gsap.timeline({ paused: true });
  tl.fromTo('#dot', { x: 0 }, { x: 400, duration: 2, ease: 'power3.out' }, 0.5);
  window.__timelines = window.__timelines || {};
  window.__timelines['scene'] = tl;
  gsap.ticker.sleep();
</script>
```

An element marked `class="clip" data-start="s" data-duration="d"` (seconds of the part) is shown only over `[s, s + d)`; the page toggles the class `hf-off` on it and leaves its own `display` alone. A part may use a timeline, `window.__partSeek`, or both (the timeline for the motion, `__partSeek` for what reacts to the beat: `beat.position`, `beat.word`); if it uses neither it is refused as always for not moving. The part checks add these rules: no `repeat: -1` on a timeline (the clock seeks, an endless repeat is not needed and hides a part that only loops); no tween of `display`, `visibility` or `autoAlpha` on a clip (animate a child of it); no CSS `transform` in a stylesheet on an element a timeline moves (set the start inside the tween with `gsap.set` or `gsap.fromTo`); no GSAP plugin (only the core is embedded: no `SplitText`, `ScrollTrigger`, `MotionPathPlugin`…, the check names the one used). A clip that ends after the scene does is only logged as a warning. Where GSAP is not installed, a part that needs it fails its check saying `bash scripts/get-gsap.sh`, and the catalog marks a part that registers a timeline with `timeline: true`. The writers of scenes and of part drafts are told this contract and these rules of movement: entrances ease out and exits ease in and are shorter, a hold of at least a beat before a hit, nothing linear but a steady drift, one punch-in on the strongest beat of the scene and every change caused by a word, a beat or a section; they are rules of the instruction, the review's criteria are the same six.

The illustrated library comes with the characters `bot`, `scientist` and `mascot` (a chibi with big headphones: poses `idle`, `wave`, `point` and `cheer`, expressions `happy`, `sad` and `surprised`) and the backgrounds `stage`, `night-sky`, `paper` and `pastel-dots`; all of them are drawn with the design's colours.

### Importing HyperFrames blocks as parts

[HyperFrames](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes) (HeyGen, Apache-2.0) has about 200 ready-made blocks (lower thirds, transitions, overlays, title cards, charts, backgrounds) that speak the timeline contract above. `npx hyperframes add <name>` installs a block as `compositions/<name>.html` on a machine with Node (the server never runs `npx` and never fetches blocks), and the program converts what is installed:

```bash
veritmo parts import-hyperframes [--only a,b] [--dry-run] [--json] [--version V] <dir>
```

`<dir>` is the folder that holds `compositions/` (or the files themselves, or the registry's `<name>/<name>.html` with its `registry-item.json`). It needs no running server: it uses the same parts folder and the same checks the server uses (and Chrome or Chromium). Each block is converted into the part `hf-<name>`: the block's page becomes a fragment in a stage the size of its canvas (1920×1080 by default), scaled to the frame and centred; the GSAP the block loads from a CDN is dropped (the page embeds the installed one); the rules about `html`, `body` and `*` stay inside the stage; every `font-family` becomes `var(--display)` (a heavy weight or a display family) or `var(--body)`, because only the design's two fonts are embedded; the variables the block declares (`data-composition-variables` or `data-variables`) become parameters (`string` with a limit of 80 characters or the length of its default, `number` with its `min` and `max`, `boolean`, `select` as an enum, a colour as a string that defaults to `var(--bg)`, `var(--fg)` or `var(--accent)` by its name) and an element with `data-var-text` reads its parameter; colour literals stay as the block wrote them and are counted as a warning. The block's `@font-face` rules are dropped before anything else is judged, so a font file under `assets/` refuses nothing (the design's fonts are used). **Small pictures travel inside the part:** a file the block reads from `assets/` as an `<img src>`, an SVG `<image href>` or a CSS `url()` is found next to the block (the registry's layout) or in `<dir>/assets/` (the layout that shares one folder), and written into the part as a `data:` URL: an SVG of at most 50 KB that passes the same check as the theme's drawings (no script, event handler, `<foreignObject>`, `@import` nor reference outside the file; one that fails refuses the block, naming the file), or a PNG, JPEG or WebP of at most 200 KB. A part carries at most 600 KB of pictures (counted after encoding; a file used twice is embedded twice) and 1 MiB of `part.html`; a file that is missing, too big or not what its name says refuses the block naming it. A block with a raster gets the warning that the picture keeps its own colours and the design does not recolour it; the manifest says `pictures: <n>`, the catalog lists it so the planner can prefer parts that follow the design, and `origin.json` lists the files (`name`, `kind`, `bytes`; the licence is the block's, Apache-2.0). The command prints `pictures: stroke.svg 12 KB, paper.webp 140 KB` under the line of such a block (`pictures: [{name, kind, bytes}]` with `--json`). A block that loads another script, mounts media (`<video>`, `<audio>`, a `<picture>` with sources, a `srcset` or `poster`, a GIF, Lottie JSON, a 3D model, an `<img>` whose source is not an embedded picture), draws on a canvas, reads any other file of `assets/`, uses the network, mounts another composition, times itself with an expression or has a vertical canvas is refused saying why, and so is one with no timeline. What converts is then checked like any part (it loads, it moves somewhere over the scene, the same frames twice, the text inside the frame and not over the page's verse, and the rules of timelines) and is written into the user's parts only if it passes, with an `origin.json` (`source: hyperframes`, the block, its version, `Apache-2.0` and the address). The command prints one line per block, `written` or `refused: <reason>`, the warnings under it, and exits 0 when at least one block was written (with `--dry-run`, would be) and 1 when none. It never replaces a part that exists. A block plays from the start of the scene it is used in and its timeline holds its last frame afterwards.

### Adding a character or a background

The illustrated style takes its characters and backgrounds from the ones that come with the program and the user's, which live in the database. Add, replace and delete the user's with `POST /api/music-motion/library/characters` (and `.../backgrounds`), `PUT .../{name}` and `DELETE .../{name}`: the body is `{manifest, files}`, the manifest as an object and the text of each drawing by file name. The drawing passes the checks below (a script, a `foreignObject`, an event handler or a load from outside is `422` with the problem and nothing is saved), `409` is a name that exists, `403` is changing or deleting one that comes with the program. Their drawings are read with `GET /api/music-motion/library/characters/{name}` and `.../backgrounds/{name}`, and the lists (`GET .../library/characters`, `.../backgrounds`) take `q`, `sort` and `limit`/`offset`. The folders of `~/.config/veritmo/music-motion/library/` that exist are imported once when the server starts (an item that is not valid is left out and named in the import record); one put there later needs `veritmo data import music-motion/library`. The format of the folder below is still the one an item is checked against.

```
library/characters/my-character/manifest.json   idle.svg  wave.svg ...
library/backgrounds/my-background/manifest.json my-background.svg
```

A character's manifest:

```json
{"name": "my-character", "description": "...", "version": 1, "width": 160, "height": 250,
 "anchor": {"x": 0.5, "y": 1.0},
 "poses": {"idle": "idle.svg", "wave": "wave.svg"}, "default_pose": "idle",
 "expressions": ["neutral", "happy"], "default_expression": "neutral"}
```

Each pose is one SVG. An expression is a group inside the SVG with the classes `expr expr-<name>` (for example `<g class="expr expr-happy">...</g>`); the one the stage asks for is shown and the others hidden. Draw with `var(--accent)`, `var(--fg)` and `var(--bg)` so the character follows the design. The anchor is the point of the drawing that goes to the position given (`0.5, 1.0` is the middle of the feet). A drawing may not contain a script, an event handler or anything loaded from outside. A background manifest is `{"name", "description", "version", "file"}`.

A piece of the script puts them on a stage:

```json
"stage": {"background": "stage", "characters": [
  {"character": "bot", "pose": "wave", "expression": "happy", "action": "bounce", "x": 30, "y": 78, "size": 42}]}
```

`x` and `y` are percentages of the frame (where the anchor goes), `size` the character's height as a percentage of the frame's height. Actions, all timed from the beat: `enter` (slides in during the first beat), `exit` (slides out in the last), `bounce`, `look` (sways), `wave` (sways twice a beat) and `slide`. A character may not be drawn partly outside the frame unless it enters or exits.

## References and styles

The videos you give as examples of a good piece can be kept as **references**: an address (the same YouTube addresses the sources of a project accept, with no account and no cookies) or an uploaded video, with a title, notes and tags. The server analyses each one in the background and keeps only its key frames and the analysis, never the video, and never uses a reference to generate pictures or video. From the command line, `veritmo references add <address>...` and `veritmo references list` use the same routes of the running server (`--server`, `--token`).

The analysis is made with fixed settings, so the same video gives the same analysis: the duration, the frame rate and the size; 12 to 24 key frames spread over the video (almost equal neighbours are dropped); the five dominant colours of the whole video and of each third (a deterministic k-means in Lab); the shots (see "The breakdown" below), the number of cuts (one less than the shots) and the mean length of a shot; and, when there is sound, the tempo and the key of the first three minutes. When a vision model is available the analysis also has a short description of the look, written by the descriptor that describes references and with its safeguards (every frame goes through the age check: a person who is, or may be, a minor stops the description and deletes that frame; a model that declines is reported, never replaced by an invented text). Without a model the description is absent and `description_note` says why.

A **style** is drawn from a reference that is `ready`: a design (the dark and light tones and the accent come from the dominant colours; an accent that does not contrast enough with the background is lightened or darkened until it does and the style says so in `adjustments`), the fonts and the finishes of the verse and the word hit from what the description says, a rhythm (the mean length of a shot, between 2 and 15 seconds) and a list of catalog parts that suit it, chosen by the assistant and checked against the catalog (empty without an assistant). A style is always valid, can be edited, and is kept when its reference is deleted. Make a piece with it by passing `preset`: its design starts as the style's (the `design` of the request wins if it is given), and the fixed rule takes the style's parts in turn and cuts the sections in pieces of the style's rhythm. A piece made without `preset` is made as always; later edits of a style do not change the pieces already made.

### The breakdown: how a reference is cut

What separates a good music video from a slideshow with the same colours is **how it is cut and how its text moves**, and the analysis measures it shot by shot, with no model:

- **Shots.** The boundaries are of two kinds. A **hard cut** is found by ffmpeg's scene-change filter at a fixed threshold (0.3). A **change** is found by comparing small grey pictures of the whole video taken twice a second: a difference that reaches three times the video's own noise (the median difference, never under 0.06) and is a peak is a change, and it is put at the picture where the picture jumps most (a video is decoded at 30 pictures a second for it, up to 30 minutes; longer ones keep the middle of the half second). A word swapped for another on the same background is a change, a slow pan is not. A change within a quarter of a second of a hard cut is that cut. A shot starts at a boundary and says which kind it was (`kind`: `cut` or `change`). With more than 600 shots the change threshold is raised first (3, 5, 7, 9), then the change pass is dropped, then the scene threshold goes to 0.4 and 0.5. A shot under 0.4 s is a flash and is merged into the one before it. Each shot has its start and end, **how much it moves** (from 0 to 1: four small grey pictures from inside the shot, mean absolute difference between consecutive ones; under 0.02 counts as static), its **dominant colour** and, when the sound has a clear tempo, whether its boundary falls within 60 ms of a **beat** (followed over the first three minutes with the same tracker as the song map). The pictures are measured and thrown away.
- **The rhythm sheet** (`analysis.rhythm`) is made from the shots: boundaries per minute in each third of the video (`cuts_per_minute`, both kinds; `changes_per_minute` says how many of them are changes and `hard_cut_share` the share that are hard cuts, low for a typographic video), the time of the first cut, the 10-second window with most cuts (the burst), the longest shot of the last two thirds (the hold), the median and the longest shot, the median in each third, the share of cuts on the beat with the `beat_phase` it was measured at, and the share of static shots. `analysis.rhythm_text` writes it as one short paragraph, the same text for the same sheet: it is what the assistants read.
- **The reading.** When a vision model is routed to describing, up to 40 shots spread over the video are read, in the background, after the reference is `ready` (`analysis.breakdown_state`: `pending`, `reading`, `done`, `skipped` with the reason, `failed`). Each gets one closed question about its middle picture and its first one: what fills the screen (`typography`, `figure`, `chart`, `ui`, `photo`, `abstract`, `empty`), how much text and how big, how it entered (`cut`, `fade`, `slide`, `scale`, `letter`, `word`) and the camera. It has the safeguards of the look: every picture goes through the age check (a person who is, or may be, a minor stops the reading and every picture of the shots is deleted), a model that declines is reported and never replaced, and an answer that is not the JSON asked for is asked once more and then recorded as `unknown`. With the readings the sheet adds the share of text-led shots and how the text enters, and its paragraph says so. The pictures stay with the reference like its key frames.
- **Running it again.** The video is not kept, so a reference made from an address gets its shots again with `POST /api/music-motion/references/{id}/breakdown {read?}`, which fetches the video once more (the same download as the analysis) and runs the same steps; a reference analysed before shots existed gets them this way and nothing is recomputed on its own. One made from an uploaded file has no address and cannot be broken down again.
- **A piece that follows it.** `reference` (the id of a `ready` reference) on `POST /api/music-motion/pieces` and on `.../propose` is kept with the piece: the design and the script proposals are given the sheet's paragraph and the median shot of each third, the fixed rule of the script cuts the pieces of each third of the song at about the reference's median shot of that third (between two beats and eight beats, whole lines, on the beat), and the scene writer is told, as a cue and never as something to copy, what the reference's shot at the same place of its video was like (its length, whether it cut on the beat and, when it was read, what was on screen, the text, how it entered and the camera). The frames, the words and the colours of the reference are not given to the assistants. A reference with no sheet is accepted and the proposal's `notes` say so.

The scene filter misses a soft cut (a crossfade reads as one longer shot, and so does a change that is a fade) and the sheet tolerates it because it reports medians. A busy picture under the text (particles, a camera that never stops) raises the video's own noise and hides the swaps, so it keeps a long shot that the eye reads as several; the hard cuts are still there and the sheet is no worse than a scene filter alone.

**The share of cuts on the beat is as good as the beat tracker**, and a tracker can lock on the off-beat. The share is therefore measured against the tracked grid and against it shifted by a quarter, a half and three quarters of a beat, and the shift that puts most boundaries on the beat is used (only when at least 8 boundaries fall in the tracked span; `beat_phase` says which, and the text says "half a beat off the tracked grid"). Read it as "cuts on a beat grid", not as a judgement of the editing. **Chance is about 30 %:** with a shift chosen out of four, boundaries placed with no regard for the music reach 29 to 30 % (the live-action example below, which is not cut to its music), so anything under about 35 % is no evidence of a pulse. A change is a weaker witness than a hard cut: a word usually arrives with an entrance of its own, so the editor's beat falls a little before or after the first picture that differs. In the typographic example the hard cuts alone are 53 % on the beat at a half-beat shift and the mix with its changes 33 %.

**A worked example.** The breakdown of one of the references of the library (a 4 min 5 s typographic music video with long camera moves, a tempo of 133 bpm), without the reading (no vision model was available): 63 shots (35 of them changes, 44 % of the boundaries hard cuts), `cuts_per_minute` `[13.2, 16.2, 16.2]`, `changes_per_minute` `[5.1, 12.5, 8.1]`, `median_shot_by_third` `[1.2, 1.8, 1.6]`, a `burst` at 1:03–1:13 with 11 cuts, a `hold` of 32.6 s that starts at 2:39 (a camera that climbs a tower of doors without a cut: a real long take), `static_share` 0.16, a `beat_phase` of 0.5 and 33 % on the beat, and the text

> Cuts and text changes: 13.2, 16.2, 16.2/min by thirds (56 % text changes); first cut at 1.5 s; burst at 1:03–1:13 (11 cuts); longest hold 32.5 s at 2:39; median shot 1.7 s; 33 % of cuts on the beat (half a beat off the tracked grid); 16 % of shots static.

*Before the change pass* the same video gave 28 shots, `cuts_per_minute` `[8.1, 3.7, 8.1]`, a `hold` of 55.3 s that started at 2:21 and 0 % of cuts on the beat: *Cuts: 8.1, 3.7, 8.1/min by thirds; first cut at 32.5 s; burst at 1:07–1:17 (10 cuts); longest hold 55.3 s at 2:21; median shot 1.4 s; 0 % of cuts on the beat; 14 % of shots static.*

What stays: the live-action look gains 13 % in shots, the typographic videos stop being one or a few shots, and the beat share of the three that are not cut to their music reads as chance (26 to 33 %) instead of a number that looks like a result.

The reading is the part that tells a typographic video from a slideshow: with it the same paragraph also says what share of the shots are text filling the frame and how that text enters.

## Drafts of parts

A new part does not have to be written by hand. Ask for a **draft** with a sentence («a gold clock that ticks on every beat») and, if you want, a picture: a key frame of a reference of the library or a file you upload. The picture is optional: with only the sentence the part is written from it alone and the draft says `with_picture: false`. A picture goes through the age check of the descriptor that describes references first (a person who is, or may be, a minor stops the draft and the picture is not kept), is described by the vision model, and that description and your sentence go to the assistant of the motion graphics, which writes the two files of a part.

A draft passes the same checks as the parts that come with the program (`CheckPart`): the manifest rules, the fragment rules (nothing from outside, no `window.__seek`), the checks of a motion page (it loads, no text cut by the edge of the frame), it does not stay the same frame all over a beat (judged with no verse on the page) and two renders give the same frames. An answer that fails is asked again with the reason, up to three attempts; when none passes the draft is `failed` and keeps the last files and problems, so you can edit them. A draft that passes has a preview of four frames over a beat.

A draft is **not** in the catalog. Only approving it copies the part to `~/.config/veritmo/music-motion/parts/<name>/` and deletes the draft; nothing approves a draft by itself. A draft made from a frame of a reference (or proposed by a learning) remembers the title and the address of that reference's video, even if the reference is deleted later; approving it writes them, with the key frame and whether it was learned, to `origin.json` next to the part, and the catalog shows them as the part's `origin`. Parts that come with the program and parts written from a sentence alone have no origin. A local model writes irregular code: the preview, the edit and the retry are there for that.

## Scenes: a part written for each verse

A music video of a quality that comes from a person's care is made of **scenes written for each verse**: one bespoke picture per line, the lyric inside the picture, one visual metaphor, one design system. A scene is an ordinary part (two files) that the assistant writes **for one script piece of one piece**, with everything that verse is: ask for it with `POST /api/music-motion/part-drafts {scene: {piece, script_piece, brief?}, model?, provider?}`. The client sends ids and, if it wants, an idea (`brief`, up to 500 characters); the server builds the writer's input from the saved piece: the script piece's lines with their words and times (in the seconds of the piece) and keyword, the hit, the section and its number, the mode, the resolved design, the length of the piece, the parts of the pieces around it (so a metaphor is not repeated) and the rubric. `request` is not needed with `scene`. The draft is checked and previewed in that verse's own lines, like any draft, and approved like any draft.

**The rubric** (the writer reads it, and the review of a scene reads the same one). These are its six rules, word for word as the writer is given them (`internal/music/musicmotion/rubric.go`):

1. One visual metaphor for what the verse MEANS (not an illustration of its literal words and not a template of bars, circles or grids), drawn from the world and the device of the video so that it belongs to them.
2. The lyric is part of the picture: the words appear at the time they are sung, in big display type (the words of the verse at least 4 vh, the word that matters at least 12 vh, nothing under 2 vh), the keyword in the accent. No labels and no captions: the only text of a scene is the verse, its keyword and the concept of the video, never a section name, a time, a counter or a route number (the HUD part draws those).
3. One accent colour and one typographic system for the whole scene: the design's own, its accent and its two fonts, and nothing else.
4. Entrances with ease and overshoot (never linear motion), something changes on every beat, depth from layers that move at different speeds.
5. Everything stays inside the frame with a margin; nothing overlaps its own text.
6. No people or faces.

**What the page checks, besides the rubric.** The rubric is judged by a model; these are measured, so a scene that breaks them is refused with the reason and written again, whatever model wrote it. Before any browser, the CSS and the SVG attributes of the scene are read: every `font-family` has to name `var(--display)` or `var(--body)`, and every colour has to be `var(--bg)`, `var(--fg)`, `var(--accent)` (or `--dark`/`--light`), a `color-mix()` or a relative colour of them, `transparent` or `currentColor`; a hex, an `rgb()` or a name such as `tomato` is refused naming it (the drawings of the theme are exempt). Then the scene is drawn once, at the middle of the sung line nearest to the middle of the scene, and the page is read: a visible word that is in none of the lines, the keyword, the hit, the concept, the idea asked for or the song's title is a caption and is refused (words of one or two letters are ignored); any text under 2 vh, a verse word under 4 vh or a largest text under 12 vh is refused; and the elements that are not text must cover at least 25 % of the frame (a grid of cells is measured, so a few dots cover what they cover and not the box around them). The writer is also given the one-sentence description of the scenes around it and of every scene of the piece already approved, under «do not repeat».

**A scene may draw the lyric itself.** A manifest may declare `"verse": "own"` (and `"hit": "own"`): the page then does **not** draw its `#verse` (or `#hitbg`/`#hit`) for a piece whose *main* part declares it, and the part reads the lines from `window.__scene.lines` (each `{text, start, end, words: [{t, s, e}], keyword, groups?}`, in the seconds of the piece) and the hit from `window.__scene.hit` (`{word, at, length}`); it may use the whole frame. An extra part (`with`) cannot declare either (`PUT /script` refuses it). The preview and the render build the same page. A part that owns the verse is also refused when a word is not drawn at its time (the frame at the start of one of the first words equals the frame just before it), and its movement is looked at with the lyric on the page. The values are `page` (the default) and `own`.

**Scene parts belong to their piece.** An approved scene is a part of yours named `scene-<short>-<n>` (`<short>` is the end of the piece's id: a part name has at most 32 characters; the piece itself is in the manifest's `scene: {piece, script_piece}`), listed by `GET /catalog` only with `?include=scene` (all) or `?piece=<id>` (that piece's), read back with `GET /parts/{name}` (so a piece exported with its parts carries it), deleted with its piece, and refused as the part of a script piece of **another** piece (`422` naming the scene and both pieces); inside its piece any script piece may use it. The proposers and the lists made for choosing do not see scene parts.

**A batch for the whole script.** `POST /api/music-motion/pieces/{id}/scenes {script_pieces?, only?: "failed", brief_mode?: "metaphor"|"literal", model?, provider?}` answers `202` and writes one draft per script piece in the background, one after the other, with the same engine (each has its previews and problems); the piece's `scenes` reports `{state, done, total, drafts: [{script_piece, draft_id, state, kept_previous?, problems?}]}`. A script piece whose attempts all failed keeps the scene it had and says so: `kept_previous: true` with the last problems (a batch of other pieces remembers it in `earlier`), `only: "failed"` writes again just those (`422` when none), and the piece review's first note lists the scenes that are older than the last batch. `409` while a proposal, a render or another batch of the piece runs; `DELETE .../scenes` stops it after the draft it is writing. Nothing is approved by itself: approve each draft as always, or all at once with `POST .../scenes/approve`, which approves every `ready` draft, sets the `part` of each script piece to its scene (its `params` and `animate` are cleared) and saves the script after validating it like `PUT /script`; it answers what it approved and what it did not, and refuses (`422`, naming the script piece) when the script changed since the batch started. A server with scenes lists `scene` in the catalog's `features`.

**Repairs before the checks.** An answer of the writer that the colour, font or clock checks would refuse is repaired by the program before it is checked: a literal colour (`#000`, `rgba(0,0,0,0.8)`, an `hsl()`) becomes the closest of `var(--bg)`, `var(--fg)` and `var(--accent)` (a translucent one keeps its opacity in a `color-mix(in srgb, var(--bg) 80%, transparent)`), a font of its own becomes `var(--display)` `Math.random()` becomes a seeded generator (`__rnd()`) defined at the start of the scene's script, and a `var()` written with letters more (`varvare(--fg)`) or a `</style>` closed with a typo (`</sc>`) is written right (`typo`). A shadow of black or white, a variable, a `url()` and a colour name other than black and white are left as they are. The draft says what it did in `repairs: [{kind: "colour"|"font"|"clock"|"typo"|"size"|"cut"|"overlap", count}]` (also in each draft of the batch report), the previews and the review see the repaired page, and an answer counts as a failed attempt only when it still fails after the repair. What only the drawn page shows is repaired after the checks have found it: when the checks name the keyword under 12 vh (`size`), a text cut by the left or right edge (`cut`) or texts that overlap (`overlap`), a small script is added at the end of the scene that, after every seek (`window.__afterSeek`, called by the page once its own layers are drawn), measures the texts as the checks do and raises the font-size of the word that matters, moves a text into the frame (with the CSS property `translate`, which does not touch the scene's own `transform`; a text wider than the frame is shrunk) or moves apart texts that cover each other, hiding first the copy of a word that the scene and the page's own hit both draw. The page is drawn and checked again and the repaired version is kept only if it passes every check; otherwise the writer's own fragment goes back to it with the problems. It does not repair text that is hidden or never appears, a page that never changes (the message asks for `window.__partSeek(t, beat)`, which is how a part moves), a script error or `Date.now()`: those go back to the writer as before.

**The fixed script.** The fixed rule that writes a script without an assistant leaves out the parts that draw example text of their own (`fixed_copy` in the catalog: a part with a text parameter whose default is text, or a block converted from HyperFrames, unless its manifest says `fixed_copy: false`), never gives the same part to two consecutive pieces, leaves out the blocks imported from HyperFrames (a background, an overlay or a transition made to go over or under another part: alone it draws nothing or covers the words), has a chorus return to its home part on its first piece and on every third one (the pieces between take the next parts in turn, never the home part or the one before) and names the pieces whose part it chose in `fallback_pieces` (in the answer of `propose`, in `script_fallback_pieces` of the background proposal and in `GET /pieces/{id}` while the script is a fallback; the piece review names, in one of its first notes, the ones that still have no scene of their own).

Scenes are written with the model you choose for each call; a small local model writes plain ones and often fails the "word at its time" rule, a strong one (a provider routed for the call) draws far better. The checks are the same for both.

## The house style: theme, fonts and the frame between pieces

What makes a finished video look finished is often in no scene: one typographic system, a HUD of tiny corner labels that never leaves, grain over everything, one glow rule, one palette. A design carries it so it reaches every page of the piece (a server that has it lists `theme` in the catalog's `features`).

**The theme** is `design.theme: {css, assets, hud}`, optional and additive (presets and datasets carry it because it is inside `design`):

- `css` (up to 20 KB) is injected **after** the page's own stylesheet in every page, for the render and for the preview, so it may restyle `#verse` and `#hit`, define the glow and the type scale and add `@font-face` from `data:` URLs. Keep it to `#verse`, `#hit`, `:root` and classes of your own (a global `*{...}` breaks parts; the preview shows the damage at once). It is refused (`422 design.theme.css: ...`) when it has `@import`, a `url(` that is not `data:`, `expression(`, `behavior:`, `javascript:` or `<`.
- `assets` (up to 5 SVG drawings of up to 50 KB each, named `^[a-z][a-z0-9_]{0,31}$`, checked like the character library: no script, event handler or outside reference) are `window.__theme.assets[name]`, and a part's fragment may place one with the slot `{{asset:name}}` (the drawing as it is). An unknown name leaves the slot empty and a draft that uses it is `ready` with a `warnings` entry saying so.
- `hud` is four texts (`tl`, `tr`, `bl`, `br`, up to 40 characters each) for the `hud-chrome` part (`window.__theme.hud`); a script piece's `design` override may replace them (`design.hud`).
- `window.__theme = {assets, hud}` is in every page, before the parts' scripts.

**Fonts.** The program carries six free fonts (SIL OFL 1.1; their licence files are in `internal/music/musicmotion/fonts/` and listed in [THIRD_PARTY.md](../THIRD_PARTY.md)): `inter`, `space-grotesk`, `jetbrains-mono`, `playfair-display`, `bebas-neue` and `fraunces`. A design that names one gets its `@font-face` embedded as a `data:` URL in every page (the weights 400 and 700, Bebas Neue only 400), so the page loads nothing from outside and the render and the dashboards' preview draw the same letters. The catalog lists them in `fonts` with their CSS in `font_stacks` and says what is embedded in `font_files` (`{family, weights, licence}`). The six names that came before still work and now map to the pack where one fits, keeping their old system stack as the fallback: `grotesk` is Inter, `mono` JetBrains Mono, `serif` Fraunces and `heavy` Bebas Neue; `rounded` and `condensed` stay system stacks. A page grows by what it embeds: about 9 KB with system stacks, 73 KB with Inter alone, 85 KB for `heavy` + `mono`, 131 KB for `inter` + `jetbrains-mono` (two weights each), cached per scene like the page.

**The frame.** Two built-in parts draw what stays across the whole song: `hud-chrome` (the four labels from the theme's `hud`, else its own `tl`, `tr`, `bl`, `br` parameters, a thin rule at the top and bottom, the section tag and a timecode, and a dot that blinks on every beat; in the body font) and `grain` (seeded film noise and slow bokeh discs, `intensity` and `bokeh` parameters; the same moment is always the same picture). Both are `manual` (the program does not choose them as a piece's part) and meant as **layer items** from 0 to the end of the song. When a proposed design has `hud` texts, the script proposal puts both on a layer each (`frame-grain` and `frame-hud`). The assistant may write `theme.css` and `hud` in a design proposal; the fixed rule writes none, and a theme that breaks its rules is left out of a design that is otherwise good.

**Crossfade between pieces.** `design.transition_seconds` (0 to 2; 0, the default, cuts as always) and a script piece's `transition_seconds` (the join *into* that piece; it wins) make the video dissolve for that long on every join, using the crossfade the video pipeline has: the generated project writes each scene's `transition_seconds`. A fade is shortened to half of the shorter of the two pieces it joins. Because the two pieces overlap during a fade, each piece is drawn as long as the fade into the next one more, so the pieces still land on the song (the video is as long as the song). The pages themselves do not change, so a dashboard blends its two frames during the overlap. Advice: 0 on a piece that lands on a word hit, 0.2 to 0.4 s elsewhere; a crossfade of a quarter of a beat reads as a dissolve on the beat.

## Reviewing scenes with the vision model

Judging whether a scene is **good** (one clear metaphor, readable type, the accent used once, nothing clipped) by eye, forty times per song, is the slow part. The program already judges frames with a vision model (the `check` route of the model routing), and the same machinery can grade a draft against the rubric the writer reads, say what is wrong in words the writer understands and ask it again before you ever see it. It is always **advice**: nothing is approved by itself.

**The rubric** is one text read twice: the writer reads it as rules and the reviewer as questions with a score of 0 (no), 1 (a little), 2 (mostly) or 3 (yes) each and a note for each one that is not a 3; the 0 to 10 score is the sum of the six scores scaled to 10 and rounded (not taken from the model; before 2026-11-15 each criterion was scored 0 to 2, so older grades are not comparable), `pass` from 7, `weak` for 5 and 6, `fail` under 5. The reviewer is also told that a label or a caption is a fault. Changing a criterion in `internal/music/musicmotion/rubric.go` changes both.

| Criterion | The writer is told | The reviewer asks |
|---|---|---|
| `metaphor` | One visual metaphor for what the verse *means* (not an illustration of its literal words and not a template of bars, circles or grids), drawn from the world and the device of the video. | Is there ONE clear visual metaphor for what the verse means, and not a generic template, and does it belong to the world and the device of the video? |
| `lyric` | The lyric is part of the picture: the words appear at the time they are sung, big display type for the words that matter and small mono labels, the keyword in the accent. | Is the lyric part of the picture instead of text pasted over it, and is it readable? |
| `accent` | One accent colour and one typographic system for the whole scene. | Is there a single accent colour and a single typographic system? |
| `motion` | Entrances with ease and overshoot (never linear motion), something changes on every beat, depth from layers that move at different speeds. | Do the frames show depth and a change from one frame to the next, as an animation and not a static slide? |
| `frame` | Everything stays inside the frame with a margin; nothing overlaps its own text. | Is every text completely inside the frame with a margin, with nothing clipped and no text over other text? |
| `people` | No people or faces. | Are there no people or faces? |

**A draft.** `review` on `POST /part-drafts` (and on `POST /pieces/{id}/scenes`, for every draft of the batch) is `true`, `false` or `{min, attempts}`; without it the setting `music_motion.review_drafts` (off by default; `PUT /api/settings {"music_motion": {"review_drafts": true}}`) decides. After the four previews are drawn the vision model of the `check` route sees them tiled in ONE picture (two by two; one call, one image, so the grade is steadier) with the draft's lines and the brief, **The reviewer describes before it scores:** it first writes one sentence per frame, as a stranger would say it, naming every object (`review.frames`), and an object that would need explaining ("those bars are mirrors") is a fault of the metaphor; when a sentence uses, as a whole word, `shape`, `shapes`, `bars`, `blob`, `abstract` or `unclear` and the world of the video (given to the reviewer, kept with the piece) does not name it, the metaphor's score is at most 1 and the first note says what the picture should have been. Its notes read `frame N · where · problem → exact change`. The draft carries `review: {score, verdict, frames, criteria: [{id, score, note}], notes (up to five concrete fixes, those of the lowest criteria first), model, attempts}`. An answer that cannot be read is asked for once more; if it still cannot, or the model fails, the draft is `ready` with `review.error` and no grade. With `min`, a draft under it is **written again with the notes as the problem** (the retry path), up to `attempts` times (2 by default, at most 3); `review.attempts` keeps every version's score and notes (the climb) and the best one is the version left `ready`, whether or not it reached the minimum. A rewrite from the review is not one of the three attempts a draft gets when its checks fail. A model that is not available, a provider without consent or a bad `min` is refused before anything is created.

**A whole piece.** `POST /api/music-motion/pieces/{id}/review` works in the background: three frames of each script piece's page (near its start, at its first word, near its end) are graded with the rubric, then one frame of each scene is tiled into a contact sheet and graded for consistency (type, palette, frame) and for scenes that repeat a metaphor; the piece reports `review: {state, scenes: [{script_piece, score, verdict, notes}], overall: {score, pictures_alone, causes_next, notes, repeated}}`; the contact sheet is also asked, with yes or no, whether the frames still tell the story with the words covered (`pictures_alone`) and whether each frame causes the next (`causes_next`), and for the looks that read as generated and the scenes that show them (the notes also name the scenes older than the last batch). About three vision calls per scene and one more. `409` while a proposal, a render or a batch of the piece runs.

Vision models grade loosely: the notes matter more than the number. Measured on this machine with `gemma4:26b` as the vision model (2026-11-13): a scene written by `qwen3:8b` got 8 (`pass`) with notes that were right about the picture (a dim word, the neon sign only suggested, text overlaid instead of integrated), and a review of a four-scene piece took about 25 seconds, grading three of the four scenes `weak` (a missing metaphor, a template, identical frames) and naming the two scenes that shared a look; the score is lenient, so use `min` with a high value (8 or 9) when you want rewrites. A strong vision model per draft doubles the time of a batch, which is why the setting is off and `min` is opt-in. A server with all of this lists `review` in the catalog's `features`.

## Craft

The prompts of the planner, the script proposal, the scene writer and the reviewer, and the skill for the agents that drive the server (`.claude/skills/music-video/`), carry one list of rules for what makes an animated music video look made by someone who has made many. **Source:** the skill references of [HyperFrames](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes) (HeyGen, Apache-2.0, at commit `3a4f57252377051f6602bd0149e84f8ead0845b2`: `story.md`, `types/music.md`, `film.md` "On music", `motion.md`, `captions.md`, `review.md`), measured by them frame by frame on their shipped films, distilled here in our own words and for our API, not copied. Each rule says whether the numbers are theirs or ours. The same list is in `.claude/skills/music-video/references/craft.md`; a test keeps the two equal.

<!-- craft:start -->
**Story** (what the planner and the scene writer are told)

- *One film:* a video has one **world** (where it happens and what it looks like, one line) and one **device** that carries it; every scene draws its metaphor from them, so the scenes belong to one film. (HyperFrames' story rules, adapted)
- *The devices* of a music video: type carries the argument; a character does the work; one object across the song; life before and after; a number lands; the result first; a question the song answers. A plan with none falls to type carries the argument. (HyperFrames' table of devices, cut down to what a song can use)
- *The hook:* the first two seconds are the densest of the video and frame 0 is the thumbnail: never black, never a logo alone. The plan says what they show, exactly. (HyperFrames)
- *One metaphor per scene* for what the verse means, not for its literal words: show what the line means, not the line. (HyperFrames; ours as the rubric's first rule)

**Edit** (what the script proposal is told)

- *Cut density follows energy:* pieces of 8–15 s in the quiet sections, one piece per two to four bars (a bar is 4 × 60 / bpm seconds) in the loud ones, one held picture on a breakdown. (HyperFrames; the numbers are ours)
- *A home part:* the chorus returns to one part, named in the answer, and escalates on each return (stronger parameters or one more extra); the ending rhymes with the opening. (HyperFrames)
- *A change inside long pieces:* a piece longer than 8 s carries something that changes inside it (an animated parameter, an extra that enters mid-piece, a layer item). A treatment goes stale after about 6 s. (HyperFrames)
- *Seams with a cause:* at most a third of the seams between pieces are hard cuts; the rest carry a transition of 0.3–0.6 s. (HyperFrames; the seconds are ours)
- *Pictures for the words:* each line gets its own picture (about 1.4–4 s) and something happens in every shot. (HyperFrames)

**Motion** (what the scene writer is told, with the numbers)

- *A landed title is dead still:* the motion moves to the next element, the camera or the world behind it. (HyperFrames, measured on their films)
- *Vary the entrances within a video* and never the same one twice in a row: per word a blur of 6 px to 0 with opacity 0 to 1 in 0.13 s, 0.067 s apart; per word from light grey to ink 0.07–0.1 s apart; the whole line blurring in from 8 px in 0.13–0.2 s; per letter for a name 0.1 s apart; one word rolling through options about 0.1 s a roll. (HyperFrames, measured)
- *Holds:* a line to be read holds 0.5 s plus 0.3 s a word, the last line at least 2 s, a one- or two-word line 0.4 s; then it leaves. (HyperFrames, measured)
- *Exits* are 40–50 % shorter than the entrance and leave with a blur-out of 0.13–0.25 s. (HyperFrames, measured)
- *Overlap and stagger:* the next element starts when the previous is 30–50 % done; letters a frame apart, words 60–120 ms. (HyperFrames, measured)
- *Ease by role:* an arrival lands on a long tail (`expo.out`, `power4.out`), a colour flood or a camera move eases both ways (`power2.inOut`), an exit accelerates (`power3.in`); nothing is linear except a steady drift. The same `power2.out` on everything is the flat default. (HyperFrames, measured)
- *Hits:* a move that lands on a hit starts 40–190 ms early, the hit itself is set with no easing, a reaction starts 0–45 ms after. (HyperFrames, measured)
- *Sizes:* the hero word fills 60–90 % of the width, quiet lines are 2.5–4 % of the height, one word in the accent. (HyperFrames; the floors the page checks are ours)
- *Generated looks,* which the review marks: a breathing, floating or pulsing loop as a title's motion; a title that keeps sliding or scaling after it lands; a slow creeping scale; bounce on text; every element fading and sliding the same way; a waveform or spectrum bar as the main picture; one treatment for more than 6 s. (HyperFrames' list of defaults that look generated and the tells of a music video)

**Captions** (the house verse)

- *Groups by energy:* a line of more than six words is shown in groups, 2–3 words in a loud section, 3–5 in a normal one, 4–6 in a calm one, breaking at punctuation or at a pause of 150 ms or more, the keyword always inside a group. (HyperFrames' caption groups; the thresholds by section are ours)
- *One group at a time:* a group opens 0.2 s before its first word (never before the line starts) and is removed 0.12 s after its last word, or when the next opens; the group with the keyword carries the accent. (HyperFrames)

**Review** (what the vision model is told)

- *Describe, then score:* one sentence per frame as a stranger would say it, naming every object; an object that would need explaining ("those bars are mirrors") is a fault of the metaphor. (HyperFrames; the score stays because the server needs it for `min` and `attempts`)
- *Notes* read `t · where · problem → exact change`. (HyperFrames)
- *The pictures alone:* with the words covered, do the frames still tell the story, and does each frame cause the next. (HyperFrames)
<!-- craft:end -->

**The house verse in groups.** A line of more than six timed words is shown in groups, one at a time, by the energy of its section (the loudest third of the song's sections: 2 to 3 words, the middle: 3 to 5, the quietest: 4 to 6), breaking at punctuation or at a pause of 150 ms or more; a group opens 0.2 s before its first word (never before the line starts) and is removed 0.12 s after its last or when the next group opens, and the group with the keyword carries the accent. The groups are in each line of `window.__scene.lines` (`groups: [{from, to, open, close}]`, words `from` to `to`, in the seconds of the piece), worked out from the song map, so two renders are the same; `design.verse_groups: false` shows every line whole, and a scene that draws the verse itself is told the same rule in the writer's instructions.

What the server does with them: the plan carries `world`, `device` and `hook`; the script proposal receives each section's loudness and energy rank, and its fixed rule follows them; the scene writer's instruction has the Motion block and the generated looks, and the review marks them; the house verse is grouped by energy (`verse_groups: false` in the design turns it off). The checks of the page (sizes, captions, colours) are still what refuses a scene; these rules are what a model is asked for.

## Learning from a reference

Making a style and asking for a part are two steps you take one by one. **Learning** does both in one call and looks at what the catalog already has, so a reference that looks like one you have already used does not give you a copy: `POST /api/music-motion/references/{id}/learn` (a `ready` reference; optional body `{"strictness": "low" | "normal" | "high"}`).

It works in the background and answers `202` with a *learning* in the state `running`; read it with `GET /api/music-motion/learnings/{id}` until it is `ready` (a reference that already has one running answers `200` with that one). It proposes **one style** (drawn like `POST /api/music-motion/presets` does) and up to **three parts**: the assistant gives ideas (a name and a sentence each), and every idea that is new is written as a normal draft, with a key frame of the reference as inspiration when there is a vision model.

Each proposal is compared with what exists: the styles for the style, the parts of the catalog and the drafts for the ideas. The distance goes from 0 (the same) to 1; for styles it weighs the accent (0.40), the dark tone, the rhythm, the finishes of the text and the parts (0.15 each), and for parts the words of the name and the description. A proposal closer than the threshold of the strictness (0.15 `low`, 0.25 `normal`, 0.40 `high`) is **not made** and goes to `discarded` with the closest thing (`similar_to`: its kind, name, distance and why) and the reason. What is kept is listed in `styles` and `part_drafts` with `novel: true` and the closest thing that exists, if any. With the 13 references of this server, `low` treats 5% of the pairs of styles as copies, `normal` 32% and `high` 85%.

Nothing enters the catalog by itself. A kept style is an ordinary style (edit it, delete it, make a piece with it) marked `origin: learned`; a kept part is a draft marked `origin: learned` that waits for you to approve it like any other. The reading of a learning shows the present state of each one: a draft still being written says `writing`, and one that no longer exists (deleted, or approved into the catalog) says `gone`.

Without an assistant, a browser, a vision model or free memory the style is still drawn, and `skipped` says what was left out and what is missing (for example `{"what": "parts", "reason": "there is no Chrome or Chromium to check and draw the part…"}`). Learning twice from one reference keeps no second copy of the style, and an idea is not kept if it is close to a draft the first call made; the assistant can still give ideas it did not give before, so each call may add up to three drafts. On this machine (the local assistant and Chrome) a learning takes about 15 seconds and its three drafts are written within about 80 seconds, in parallel; a learning that was running when the server stopped reads `failed`.

## Timing the words

The times of the words decide when a verse enters, so they are worth some work. Hearing the words (`transcribe: true`, or `POST .../transcribe`) runs `whisper-cli` with the time it computes for each token (dynamic time warping, `-dtw`, with the preset that matches the model; flash attention is turned off because it returns no token times) and builds the words from the tokens, each with a `confidence`. A model with no preset, or a run that gives no token time, falls back to one word per segment and `words_note` says so: then a word may land early or late by a good part of a second. The words of the map are always the written ones: what is heard only gives the times. A written word that nothing matches (a number, an abbreviation, a word Whisper did not hear) takes a time between its neighbours; a heard word that matches nothing is left out.

Four things make the times better when they are available:

- **The voice, separated first.** With the optional separator installed (`bash scripts/get-demucs.sh --build`, demucs.cpp on the CPU, no GPU and no Python: about 0.9 times the length of the song with four threads and about 6 GB of memory) the song is separated, Whisper reads the voice alone and the voice's envelope (voice or no voice, every 20 ms) is kept for the alignment: a word heard where nobody sings (over a drum fill) is left out and a word that was not heard is placed only where the voice is. `separate` is `auto` by default, which separates when the separator is installed and fits in memory (and hears the mix, saying why in `words_note`, when it does not), `true` separates and refuses with `503` when it cannot (not installed, no memory, a run in progress) and `false` never uses the voice. The separated voice is kept in the piece's folder as `vocals-16k.wav` (about 1.9 MB per minute) and reused when the words are heard again; it goes with the piece. A voice heard in less than 5 % of the song counts as none. The map then says `words_source: heard-voice`.
- **When the voice loses words, the mix is heard too.** A stem can lose words (a doubled or buried voice, heavy processing) and the alignment cannot recover what Whisper never wrote. So, when the piece has written lyrics and the words Whisper heard on the stem match fewer than 75 % of the written words (counted the way the alignment counts them, with the voice's envelope), the server also reads the mix and keeps the hearing that matches more written words (the voice on a tie). The envelope and the attacks stay the stem's in both cases, so the words are still gated and placed with the voice; the map says `words_source: heard-voice` and `words_note` says "the separated voice heard 53 of 95 written words and the mix 91: the words were heard on the mix and placed with the voice's attacks". It costs one more Whisper run (about 30 s for two minutes of song) only in that case; without written lyrics there is nothing to count against and it never runs. The 75 % sits in the gap of the bench (see "Fallback to the mix" below): the song that lost words had 57 % of them matched on the stem, the next lowest 78 % and the rest 89 % or more.
- **The attacks of the voice.** From the separated voice the server finds where a syllable starts (the spectral flux of the voice, in the band of the voice, above an adaptive threshold, at least 80 ms apart and only where the voice is heard: about one attack per 280 ms of voice, 170 to 230 for a song of 90 s) and a heard word that starts within 120 ms of one is moved onto it, keeping its length, unless that would put it within 40 ms of its neighbour. Such a word says `snapped: "attack"` and the map says how many attacks it found (`attacks`). This is the step that puts the words on the syllable rather than near it.
- **The beat.** A heard word that was not moved onto an attack and starts within 60 ms of a beat (of the half beat in a song under 100 bpm) is moved onto it, keeping its length, unless that would take it past a neighbour (`snapped: "beat"`). A word that was not heard is never moved, and nothing is snapped to the beat when the beats could only be placed on a plain grid. With no separated voice there are no attacks and this is the only snap.
- **A hand.** `PATCH /api/music-motion/pieces/{id}/words` moves words where you hear them. The map then says `words_source: edited` and the moved words `heard: false`.

`song_map.words_source` is `estimated` (the lines were spread over their sections), `heard`, `heard-voice` or `edited`; each word says whether it was heard (`heard`) and how sure Whisper was (`confidence`, 0 when it was not heard).

### What was measured

`veritmo words bench <projects-dir> [--songs n] [--out report.json]` takes the songs with written lyrics of a music project (or of a folder of them), separates the voice of each once and finds its attacks, which are the stick: where a syllable starts. It hears the words four ways: one word per segment on the mix (how they were timed at first), the time of each token on the mix, the time of each token on the separated voice, and the same words with the ones near an attack moved onto it. Per way, for the heard words inside the written span (from the first to the last word, a second each side), it reports the shares whose start is within 50, 100 and 150 ms of an attack, the median distance to the nearest attack (within 300 ms) and the signed median (the lag: positive when the words start before the attacks), the attacks with no word start within 300 ms (voice that is not in the lyrics), the share of word starts within 60 ms of a beat next to the share a random start would get at that tempo, and the seconds taken. The JSON keeps every word and the attacks, so a report can be measured again without the audio. Without the separator it says it cannot measure and exits 0.

The decision rule: `separate: auto` separates by default only if, over at least 8 songs with lyrics of their own (songs with the same lyrics are variants of one track and count once), moving the words onto the attacks lifts the share of heard words within 50 ms of an attack by at least 20 points on the median song and the whole voice way (separation, attacks and Whisper) takes under three times the length of the song. With fewer songs it prints the numbers and `decision: not decided`.

An earlier version of the bench measured the words against the *onsets* of the voice, the rises of a smoothed envelope after a pause: those are phrase starts (9 to 29 per song for 46 to 177 words), not syllables, so what it counted was something else and its table was replaced.

Run on 2026-11-14 on an Intel Core Ultra 7 265 (20 threads, no GPU used), model `ggml-large-v3-turbo-q5_0`, over **10 distinct songs of 90 s** made with HeartMuLa for the purpose (different lyrics, styles and tempos from 79 to 171 bpm; the report is in `openspec/changes/archive/*-snap-words-to-attacks/report/`). Medians over the songs:

| way | heard words | within 50 ms | within 100 ms | within 150 ms | distance | lag | attacks with no word | on beat (chance) | seconds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| one word per segment, mix | 102 | 28.4 % | 48.7 % | 62.5 % | 72 ms | +2 ms | 52 | 19.6 % (21.3 %) | 21.6 |
| tokens, mix | 102 | 28.3 % | 56.0 % | 76.1 % | 80 ms | -18 ms | 23 | 18.6 % (21.3 %) | 28.1 |
| tokens, separated voice | 101 | 26.4 % | 55.2 % | 75.6 % | 83 ms | -13 ms | 25 | 18.0 % (21.3 %) | 110.2 |
| tokens, separated voice, moved onto the attacks | 101 | 62.0 % | 63.1 % | 75.6 % | 0 ms | 0 ms | 29 | 21.0 % (21.3 %) | 110.2 |

What it says, and what it does not:

- **The decision is `auto` separates**: moving the words onto the attacks lifts the words within 50 ms of an attack by 33.7 points on the median song (the rule asks for 20) and the voice way takes 1.22 times the length of the song (under 3).
- **That gain is by construction.** A word moved onto an attack is within 50 ms of it, so the 62.0 % counts how many words were moved (and so how many had an attack within 120 ms and a free neighbourhood), not whether each went to *its own* syllable. There is no ground truth in this bench: it cannot say how many of the moves are right.
- **The separation alone did not improve the timing**: before the snap the voice is at 26.4 % within 50 ms and the mix at 28.3 %, 55.2 % and 56.0 % within 100 ms. What the voice gives is the attacks (and a cleaner read in some songs); what the tokens give over the segments is in the 100 and 150 ms columns (56 % and 76 % against 49 % and 63 %) and in the attacks left without a word (23 against 52).
- **Whisper is neither systematically early nor late**: the lag is within -18 and +2 ms, a few milliseconds against a spread of 70 to 100 ms, so there is no constant to correct.
- **The voice way heard fewer words in some songs** (53 of 91 in `baile_lento`, the soft R&B one, where words heard over a quiet voice were left out as unvoiced) and each word it leaves out gets a made-up time between its neighbours.
- **Beats**: the share of word starts within 60 ms of a beat is at the level of chance (about 20 % against 21 %), so the beat snap moves words to places no more likely to be right; it only keeps what the attacks did not move.
- **Fallback to the mix** (`hear-mix-when-voice-loses-words`, bench of 2026-11-15 on the same ten songs): the test of the paragraph "When the voice loses words" triggers on **2 of 10 songs**. `baile_lento`: the stem matched 52 of 91 written words (57 %) and the mix 91 of 91, so the mix is kept. `isla_calma` (171 bpm): the stem matched 62 of 105 (59 %) and the mix the same 62, so the voice is kept and the extra Whisper run (about 30 s) was for nothing: the lyrics have words the voice does not sing, not a stem that lost them. The next lowest, `carta_sin_enviar`, matches 96 of 123 (78 %) and is left alone; the other seven match 89 % or more. The bench reports the counts per song (`fallback: voice matched 52/91, mix 91/91 → mix`), the JSON keeps them in `fallback: {voice, mix, written, kept}` and the summary counts the songs where it triggers. The report was kept with the benchmark output (`words-bench-fallback`). On the real server the stem of `baile_lento` matched 79 of 91 words (87 %), not 52: the stem the server separates and the one the bench separates are not the same bytes, and Whisper reads them differently, so this song sits at the edge and the fallback did not trigger there (the mix gave 91 of 91; the trigger itself is tested with a Whisper that answers differently per file). `isla_calma` on the server: 62 of 105 on the voice and 62 on the mix, a tie, the voice kept.
- **The cost**: 110 s of hearing for 90 s of song (the separation is 82 s of it) against 28 s on the mix. `separate: false` is the fast way; the constant `separateByDefault` in `internal/server/music/musicmotion.go` flips the default.

To measure again: `veritmo words bench projects/<a music project> --out words-bench.json`; `wordsbench.Recompute` measures a saved report again without the audio.

| `PUT /api/music-motion/parts/{name}` · `DELETE /api/music-motion/parts/{name}` | Change or remove a part of yours without going through a draft. `PUT {manifest, fragment}` checks them like an approval (the manifest keeps the name; `422` with the problems and nothing changes) and keeps the part's origin. `DELETE` answers `204`, or `409` naming the pieces and styles that use the part unless `?force=true`. Both give `409` for a part that comes with the program, `404` for one that does not exist; the catalog shows the change at once. |
| `GET/PUT/DELETE /api/music-motion/presets/{id}` | Read a style, replace it (`{name, design, rhythm, parts}`, validated: `422` naming the field, for example `design.accent`) or delete it. A style stays when its reference is deleted. |

A piece is a hidden project (`api-mm-...`): it is not in `GET /api/projects` or the per-project statistics; its runs count under the `client` it was created with.

## Development

The engine is `internal/music/musicmotion` (design, catalog, parts, script, stage, project and estimate), `internal/music/songmap` (the song map) and the beat clock is in `internal/video/motion/beatclock.go`. Tests that need a browser or ffmpeg skip when they are missing. To look at the art while writing parts: `DUMP_PARTS_DIR=/some/dir DUMP_PARTS_AT=2.3 go test ./internal/music/musicmotion -run TestDumpParts` writes a frame of every part, `TestDumpStages` of the stage, `DUMP_VIDEOS_DIR` renders three demo videos and `MEASURE_PARTS=1 ... -run TestMeasureRenderTimes -v` measures the render times.
